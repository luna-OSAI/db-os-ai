# Objections — the agency founder

> **`inferred`.**

## The difference that changes everything

**He is not afraid of the platform — he will interrogate it.** He is deciding on arithmetic: margin,
capacity, repeatability, and whether it survives contact with real clients.

**Do not sell him ease. Sell him leverage.** And do not oversell — he will test it, and losing
credibility with this buyer is unrecoverable.

---

## "Isn't this just GoHighLevel?"

**The hardest objection in the database.** A meaningful share of agency founders will recognise the
interface, and this question will come.

**The rule stands: never name it.** But never deny that a third-party platform is involved either —
denial is a false statement, and he will know.

> "We license and configure enterprise infrastructure rather than rebuilding what already works.
> What you'd be buying isn't the software layer — it's the agent builds, the automation library,
> the setup process and the support. If you're already running your own stack and you're comfortable
> implementing, honestly, you might not need us."

**That last sentence is the one that works.** Offering the disqualification is what makes the rest
credible. [White-label disclosure](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/white-label-disclosure.md).

## "I could build this myself"

> "You probably could. The question is whether you want to keep maintaining it. What's it costing
> you in time across all your clients right now?"

**Do not argue he cannot.** He can. The argument is opportunity cost.

## "What are the margins?"

Commission percentages are contractual facts and may be stated: 40% at Pro, 45% plus 5% at Partner,
50% plus 30% plus 10% at Platinum.

> ⚠️ **Percentages only. Never dollars. Never a projection. Never "you could earn…"**
> [Partner and income claims](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/partner-and-income-claims.md).

## "Can I white-label it for my clients?"

> **Not documented.** Whether agencies can rebrand for their own clients is not recorded anywhere in
> the source material. **Say you'll confirm.** Do not assume the answer is yes.

## "What happens if you go under?"

Fair and serious — he would be betting client operations on a five-person company.

> "Reasonable question. Your data is exportable, and the underlying infrastructure is enterprise
> platform, not something we wrote. But I'm not going to pretend a five-person company carries the
> same continuity profile as HubSpot — that's a real thing to weigh."

**Honesty here is the only option.** He has already thought of it.

## "Your integration list is thin"

> "It is, compared to a mature platform. What specifically do you need to connect?"

Then check whether it exists. **Never claim an integration you have not confirmed.**

## "What about SSO / SLA / security review?"

> **None documented.** "That's not something we have documentation for. If your clients need a
> security review, we're not the right fit yet."

**A clean disqualification.** Better than a vague answer that collapses under procurement.

## "How is this different from other GHL resellers?"

He may ask directly. Do not name the category.

> "The setup and the agent builds. Most of the market sells you access and a template library. What
> we do is the implementation — which is the part that actually determines whether any of it works."

**True, and it is genuinely the differentiator.** [Positioning](../04-voice-and-messaging/positioning.md).

## Never

- Overclaim technical capability — he will test it
- Deny a third-party platform is involved
- State any income projection
- Claim an integration without confirming
- Imply enterprise readiness that does not exist
