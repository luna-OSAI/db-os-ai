# Brand voice

## The one-line description

**The no-BS wizard.** Someone who can genuinely do the impossible-looking thing, and refuses to be
mystical about it.

That tension is the whole voice. Magician energy — transformation, systems that feel like magic —
delivered in the flattest, most Australian, least precious register available. The promise is
**"The Magic Is In The System"**: the wonder is real, and it lives in the plumbing.

## The rules

### 1 · Australian English, always
Organise, optimise, realise, prioritise. Programme is programme. No z-spellings. Idiom is welcome
where it is natural — "sorted", "flat out", "no worries" — and forced where it is not.

### 2 · Lead with the answer
Then the reasoning. Never build to a conclusion the reader has to wait for. This is the Ruler
30%: authority reads as getting to the point.

### 3 · Short sentences. Then a longer one when the idea needs room to breathe.
Vary it. Uniform sentence length is the most reliable tell of generated text.

### 4 · Quantify wherever the number is real
"Replaces Mailchimp, Calendly, ClickFunnels and Zapier" beats "replaces your stack." But never
invent the number — see [claims policy](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/claims-policy.md).

### 5 · Name the mechanism
Magician brands fail by being vague. The wonder has to be specific or it reads as snake oil — which
is literally the Magician's shadow archetype. *How* it works is the proof that it works.

### 6 · Talk to one person
Second person, contractions, direct address. Never "businesses like yours." Never "clients often
find."

### 7 · Never condescend
Ethan is not stupid, he is busy and non-technical. Explaining is fine; talking down is fatal.

### 8 · Never say "it's easy"
Explicitly banned by the company. It insults the reader's experience and it is not true — the setup
is genuinely the hard part, which is why the done-for-you line exists.

### 9 · Outcome before feature
The Magician sells what changes, not what the product does. Feature lists are Ruler drift.

### 10 · Confidence without hype
Authority comes from precision, not volume. If a sentence needs an exclamation mark to land, the
sentence is wrong.

## The 70/30 blend in practice

| Magician (70%) — the argument | Ruler (30%) — the delivery |
|---|---|
| Transformation, before/after, what becomes possible | Structure, order, control, competence |
| "Turn manual follow-up into a system that runs itself" | "Ten steps. Named owners. A QA gate before anyone calls it done" |
| Vision-led headlines | Precise, checkable body copy |
| The customer is the hero; OS A.I is the guide | OS A.I is unmistakably competent |

**Practical ratio:** Magician in the headline and the promise. Ruler in the proof and the process.
A page that is all Magician reads as hype; all Ruler reads as a manual.

## The test sentences

Three checks on any draft:

1. **Would Tumai say this out loud, to a business owner he respects, without wincing?**
2. **Does it name a mechanism, or just assert a transformation?**
3. **Could a competitor put their logo on it?** If yes, it is category copy, not brand copy.

## Rhythm

- Paragraphs: 1–3 sentences in short-form, up to 4 in articles
- **Fragments are allowed.** Used deliberately, sparingly
- Headings are sentence case, not Title Case
- Bold for the load-bearing sentence in a section, not for decoration
- No emoji in copy. No exclamation marks

## Two named voices

**Tumai** — vision, systems, positioning, market argument. First person. The one who zooms out.
**Ariki** — sales, delivery, what breaks in practice. First person. The one who has done it.

Attribute deliberately. A piece about the economics of tool sprawl is Tumai. A piece about what
goes wrong in week one of onboarding is Ariki.
