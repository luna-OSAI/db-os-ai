## The published structure

Live site heading: **"Unlock the Advantages of Becoming a Global Brand Partner."**

| Plan | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pro** ($97/mo) | 40% | — | — |
| **Partner** ($297/mo) | 45% | 5% | — |
| **Platinum** ($497/mo) | 50% | 30% | 10% |

Partners earn on their own referrals (tier 1) and, at higher plans, on referrals made by partners
they introduced (tiers 2 and 3).

## Why this needs care

A commission structure that pays on the recruits of your recruits is **multi-level**. That is legal
in Australia and unremarkable in software reselling — but it sits inside a regulatory perimeter that
ordinary affiliate programs do not.

The relevant boundaries, in plain terms:

- **Pyramid schemes are prohibited** under the Australian Consumer Law (Sch 2, Part 3-1, Div 3). The distinguishing feature is whether payments flow principally from **recruitment** rather than from the **sale of a genuine product**. OS A.I sells a real platform at a real price, which is the right side of the line — but recruitment-led marketing language can blur it.
- **Referral selling is prohibited** (ACL s49) where a sale is induced by promising a rebate or commission contingent on the buyer supplying names of other prospects.
- **Any earnings representation** — stated, implied or illustrated — is a claim requiring substantiation. **No partner earnings data exists** in the source set.

None of this makes the program a problem. It makes *how it is marketed* the thing that matters.

## Working rules until the compliance file is confirmed

1. **Never state or imply an income.** No dollar figures, no "replace your salary", no ranges, no illustrative examples, no screenshots.
2. **Never present partnering as an income opportunity in general-audience marketing.** It is a reseller arrangement for people already using the platform.
3. **Lead with the product.** The payout is a term of the plan, not the reason to buy it.
4. **Never imply typicality.** With no data, there is no typical.
5. **Never make recruitment the pitch.**

## What is missing

- **The Affiliate Policy has not been mapped into marketing rules.** It exists; it has not been read into a do/don't list.
- No partner count, no partner earnings distribution, no partner case study.
- No stated rule on what a partner may say when recruiting on OS A.I's behalf. **Partners are agents of the brand's compliance risk** — their claims are the company's exposure.
- No disclosure standard for partner-generated content.
