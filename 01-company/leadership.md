## Who owns what

- **Tumai** — vision, marketing, brand, offer strategy, systems architecture. Final say on positioning.
- **Ariki** — sales, operations, delivery. Converts the vision into a running process.
- **Audrey** — onboarding and client success. Owns steps 2–10 of the onboarding SOP and the client relationship after handoff.
- **Jordan** — websites and funnels. Builds the client-facing surfaces.
- **Luna** — content and acquisition execution.

**Decision authority:** Tumai and Ariki. Advisory input from everyone else.

## What this means for content

**The founders are the brand.** With no case studies to lean on, founder credibility and the
quality of the argument carry the marketing. Content that sounds institutional wastes the one asset
the business actually has.

**Two voices are usable in first person.** Tumai for vision, marketing and systems thinking. Ariki
for sales, delivery and operational reality. Attribute deliberately — a piece about tool sprawl
economics reads as Tumai; a piece about what breaks in week one of onboarding reads as Ariki.

**It is a family business.** Tumai and Audrey are partners; Jordan is Audrey's son; Ariki is Tumai's
brother. This is usable as origin-story material where it is genuinely relevant, and it is a real
differentiator against faceless SaaS. It is not a reason to be sentimental in performance copy.

## Deliberately not recorded here

The source document carries MBTI types, Enneagram, Human Design, numerology, astrology, VIA
strengths, stress and burnout patterns, and personal history including homelessness and religious
exile — for all five people.

**None of it is in this database.** It is personal data about people who are not the requester, it
is not needed to write marketing copy, and a marketing knowledge base is the wrong place to keep it.
Decision D3 in [SCHEMA](../00-start-here/SCHEMA.md).

If a piece of personal story is ever needed for content, the route is to ask that person what they
are happy to have published — not to mine the profile document.
