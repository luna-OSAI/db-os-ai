# Disqualifiers

> **`inferred`.** No source document states who OS A.I turns away (gap G11). This is reasoned from
> the offer and delivery model. **Confirm with Tumai and Ariki** — a real disqualifier list usually
> comes from three bad-fit clients, and those conversations have not been captured.

Why this file matters: **a business that will sell to anyone reads as a business that has not
figured out what it is.** Naming who you turn away is one of the cheapest credibility signals
available, and it is the fastest way to shorten a sales cycle.

## Hard — decline these

| Who | Why |
|---|---|
| **Pre-revenue with no lead flow at all** | The platform automates a process. If there is no process and no enquiries, there is nothing to automate. They need customers first |
| **Anyone wanting the AI agent to make claims they cannot substantiate** | Their compliance risk becomes OS A.I's, and the agent speaks in their name |
| **Regulated verticals with prescriptive advice rules** — credit, financial advice, medical, legal — **without their own compliance sign-off** | An AI agent conversing with prospects in those categories carries real regulatory exposure that OS A.I is not positioned to carry |
| **Businesses wanting cold outbound to purchased lists** | Spam Act and DNC obligations. The platform can technically do it; OS A.I should not enable it |
| **Anyone whose main interest is the commission structure** and who has no intention of using the platform | See [partner and income claims](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/partner-and-income-claims.md). This is the single most important disqualifier for keeping the partner program clean |

## Soft — proceed carefully, expect friction

| Who | Why |
|---|---|
| Businesses with a genuinely working, integrated stack | The consolidation argument does not apply. Do not manufacture a problem |
| Enterprises needing SSO, procurement, SLAs, security review | Not documented as available. Do not imply it is |
| Anyone wanting a specific integration not confirmed to exist | Confirm before selling |
| Owners who want to build it themselves but buy the DFY tier | Mismatch. Sell them the subscription |
| Very high call-volume operations | No capacity or throughput data exists |
| Businesses outside AU/NZ/North America | Markets are stated. Support hours and compliance posture beyond them are undocumented |

## How to decline well

A good decline earns referrals; a bad one earns a review. Three rules:

1. **Be specific about why.** "You'd be paying for automation before you have enough enquiries to automate" is useful. "You're not a fit" is not.
2. **Tell them what to do instead**, even when it is not OS A.I.
3. **Leave the door open with a condition.** "Come back when you're consistently getting more enquiries than you can respond to the same day."
