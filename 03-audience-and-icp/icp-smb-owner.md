# ICP — the service-based SMB owner

**Internal name: "Entrepreneur Ethan."** The primary buyer.

> **`inferred`.** This persona is built from the team's own documented assumptions, not from
> observed prospects. It is detailed and internally consistent, and it may well be right — but no
> sentence of it comes from a real customer's mouth. **Every hook and objection derived from it
> inherits that.** Gap G5, the highest-leverage gap in the database.

## In one line

A service-based business owner drowning in manual follow-up, losing leads to slow response, and
paying for a stack of tools that do not talk to each other.

## Profile

| | |
|---|---|
| Age | 30–45 |
| Location | Australia, New Zealand, North America |
| Business | Service-based — consultant, coach, trades, professional services, small agency |
| Team | 1–10 staff |
| Income | $80K–$200K, **inconsistent month to month** |
| Education | Certificate, diploma or degree. Practical learner, **not technical** |
| Where they are | LinkedIn (primary), YouTube (education), Facebook groups |
| Tech posture | **Tech-curious, not tech-savvy.** Interested in AI, intimidated by implementation |

That last line is the whole persona. He is not resistant to AI. He is resistant to *another thing to
set up*.

## Their three problems

1. **Inconsistent lead flow** → unpredictable revenue, constant planning anxiety
2. **Manual workload with no systems** for follow-up, sales or retention
3. **Too many disconnected tools** that cost too much and do not work together

## What they want

1. Predictable leads and booked sales without chasing people
2. A simple system that works without tech overwhelm
3. Time back — without losing quality or control

**"Without losing control" is the constraint everyone forgets.** He does not want to hand his
business to a black box. Automation copy that sounds like it removes him from his own business
triggers the fear rather than the want.

## What they fear

1. The business stagnating or failing because of poor systems and missed leads
2. **Implementing the wrong tech and damaging client experience or reputation**
3. Burning out — having built a job, not a business

**Fear 2 is the one that actually blocks the sale.** He has been burned before: bought a platform,
never finished setting it up, quietly kept paying for it. The done-for-you line exists precisely
because of this fear, and copy should speak to it directly rather than adding more capability.

## What they aspire to

A stable business that runs without constant involvement · 15–20 hours a week back for family,
health and thinking · confidence that the systems support long-term growth.

## Trigger events — when they become buyable

`inferred`, but reasoned from the problem set:

- Lost a deal to slow response and **knows** they lost it that way
- A software renewal lands and they add up the stack for the first time
- Someone quits and the follow-up they were doing simply stops
- A competitor visibly out-responds them
- Missed a family event because of work — the emotional trigger
- Tried an all-in-one platform, never finished the setup, still paying

## Jobs to be done

- **Functional** — capture every enquiry, respond immediately, book without back-and-forth, stop paying for overlapping tools
- **Emotional** — stop feeling behind. Stop the low-grade dread that something is being missed
- **Social** — look like a business that has it together

## How they talk about it

From the source set — team-authored, so **`inferred`, not voice-of-customer**:

> "Flat out like a lizard drinking" · "Stuck in manual mode" · "Drowning in tools and admin" ·
> "Sick of firefighting" · "Working IN the business, not ON it" · "Built a business for freedom but
> got a 60-hour week instead"

Australian idiom is authentic here and should be used. **But these are the team's words for Ethan,
not Ethan's words.** Real language changes this file the day it arrives.

## Writing for Ethan

- **Lead with the problem, not the platform.** He does not want software; he wants the problem gone.
- **Never say "it's easy."** Explicitly banned. It insults him and it is not true.
- **Be specific about the mechanism.** Vague transformation reads as hype; a named mechanism reads as real.
- **Address the setup fear early.** Every capability you add sounds like more work until you say who does it.
- **Australian, direct, no fluff.** He is allergic to salesy.
- **Never promise a result.** He has been promised results before. See [guardrails](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/guardrails.md).
