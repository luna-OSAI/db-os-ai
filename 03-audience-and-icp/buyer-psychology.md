# Buyer psychology

## The decision is not about software

Ethan is not comparing platforms. He is deciding whether to **trust another system with the part of
his business that is currently held together by his own memory.**

That reframes everything. The competitor is not HubSpot — it is **doing nothing**, which is free,
familiar, and has not killed the business yet.

## The three states, in order

Content has to move someone through these in sequence. Skipping is what makes copy fail.

### 1 · Unaware the gap is costing money
He knows he is busy. He does not know that the deals he lost were lost in the **four hours between
enquiry and reply**, because a lost deal is invisible — it never appears in a report.

**Job of content:** make the invisible loss visible. This is the highest-value educational move OS A.I
can make and it is what the [speed-to-lead cluster](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-speed-to-lead.md) exists for.

### 2 · Aware, but paralysed by the fix
Now he knows. He also believes fixing it means learning another platform, migrating data, and three
weekends. **So he does nothing, and the awareness converts into low-grade guilt rather than action.**

**Job of content:** shrink the perceived cost of change. This is where done-for-you is the argument,
not a feature.

### 3 · Ready, but afraid of choosing wrong
He has been burned. He bought a platform, never finished configuring it, and is still paying for it.
Fear 2 in his profile — *implementing the wrong tech* — is the real blocker.

**Job of content:** de-risk the decision. Specific mechanism, named process, visible people.

## What builds trust here

| Builds it | Destroys it |
|---|---|
| Naming a problem precisely, before selling | Generic "grow your business" language |
| Admitting what the product does not do | Overclaiming, then being tested |
| Showing the mechanism | Vague transformation promises |
| Founder visibility | Faceless SaaS voice |
| Saying "that isn't a fit for us" | Selling to everyone |
| Being specific about setup | "It's easy" — banned, and he does not believe it |

## The objection underneath every objection

Almost every stated objection is a proxy for one of two real ones:

> **"I don't believe this will actually get set up."**
> — behind *"too expensive"*, *"I need to think about it"*, *"send me some info"*

> **"I don't want to depend on something I don't understand."**
> — behind *"how does the AI work?"*, *"what if it says the wrong thing?"*, *"I'd rather do it myself"*

Answering the stated objection while leaving the real one alone is how these deals stall. The
diagnosis-first sales approach in the sales plan exists for exactly this reason.

## What the agency founder decides on instead

Not trust — **arithmetic.** Margin, capacity, repeatability, whether it survives contact with real
clients. He will test the claim, so the claim has to be true.
