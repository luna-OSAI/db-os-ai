# ICP — the agency founder

The secondary buyer, and the more commercially interesting one: they buy for themselves **and** they
become a distribution channel.

> **`inferred`** — same caveat as [Ethan](icp-smb-owner.md). Built from team assumptions.

## In one line

An agency owner who generates leads but loses deals to slow, manual, inconsistent follow-up — and
needs automation to close more without hiring sales staff.

## Profile

| | |
|---|---|
| Age | 28–45 |
| Location | Australia, New Zealand, North America, UK |
| Role | Agency owner, consultant, growth strategist, automation specialist |
| Income | $100K–$500K — growing but **capped by capacity** |
| Education | Diploma or degree; largely self-taught in business, marketing and tech |
| Where they are | LinkedIn (primary), YouTube, X, Slack and Discord communities, masterminds |
| Tech posture | **Genuinely technical.** Will evaluate the platform on its merits |

## The critical difference from Ethan

**Ethan buys because he cannot do it. The agency founder buys because he does not want to do it
again for every client.**

That changes everything about how you sell:

- He is **not intimidated** by the platform — he will interrogate it
- He may **recognise the underlying software.** See [white-label disclosure](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/white-label-disclosure.md)
- He cares about **margin, leverage and repeatability**, not simplicity
- He evaluates the **partner economics** as a business line, not a perk
- He has **already tried** Zapier, Make, a CRM and probably an all-in-one — and has opinions

**Do not sell him ease. Sell him leverage.**

## Their three problems

1. **Manual, inconsistent follow-up** — leads go cold before anyone contacts them
2. **Revenue capped by delivery capacity** — cannot scale without hiring
3. **No standardisation across clients** — every account is bespoke, nothing compounds

## What they want

1. Automated qualification and booking that runs without the founder
2. **A white-label or partner solution they can resell** or fold into client offers
3. More revenue per client without more workload

## What they fear

1. Losing clients to faster, more automated competitors
2. Over-investing in tech that does not deliver measurable ROI
3. **A revenue ceiling because the business depends entirely on them**

## Trigger events

- Turned down work because delivery was full
- Lost a retainer to an agency that automated something they do by hand
- Realised they are rebuilding the same system for every client
- Looking for recurring revenue to sit under project work
- Hit the hiring decision and does not want to make it

## Jobs to be done

- **Functional** — standardise delivery, add recurring revenue, remove themselves from follow-up
- **Emotional** — stop being the constraint on their own business
- **Social** — be the automation expert in their niche, not a generalist

## Writing for the agency founder

- **Lead with leverage and margin.** Capacity, repeatability, recurring revenue.
- **Be technically specific.** Name the node types, the routing, the staging environment. Vagueness reads as weakness to someone who builds.
- **Respect what they have already tried.** Zapier is not a punchline to someone who has built with it — the argument is that stitching tools is a maintenance liability, not that automation is hard.
- **The partner economics are a legitimate part of the pitch** — bounded by [partner and income claims](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/partner-and-income-claims.md). Percentages, never dollars.
- **Never oversell simplicity.** He will test it, and losing credibility here is unrecoverable.
