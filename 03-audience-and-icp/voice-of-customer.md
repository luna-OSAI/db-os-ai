# Voice of customer

## This file is empty, and that is the most useful thing in it

**Not one sentence of real prospect or customer language exists anywhere in the OS A.I source
material.** No call recordings. No transcripts. No sales-call notes. No support tickets. No reviews.
No survey responses. No community posts.

Every persona detail, every fear, every phrase attributed to "Entrepreneur Ethan" was written by the
team **about** the customer, not captured **from** them.

## Why this is the highest-leverage gap in the database

In the reference databases this build was modelled on, the voice-of-customer file is consistently
the single most valuable asset in the repo — because it is the only place where the copy stops
sounding like a category and starts sounding like a person.

Everything downstream inherits the gap:

- Hooks are guesses about what lands
- Objection turns answer objections nobody has verified are the real ones
- Article angles target problems phrased the way the team phrases them
- The ICP files are marked `inferred` and cannot be upgraded without this

**One recorded sales call would close more of this than another month of internal documents.**

## What to capture, and how

The cheapest possible version: **record the next five discovery calls** and pull verbatim quotes.
Nothing else needed — no research budget, no survey tool.

What to extract:

| Capture | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **How they describe the problem, unprompted** — the first 60 seconds | This is the hook. Never paraphrase it |
| **The exact words for the cost of the problem** | "I found out three weeks later" beats any headline you would write |
| **What they have already tried** | Kills copy that pitches something they have rejected |
| **The objection that arrives unprompted** | The real one, not the polite one |
| **What made them book the call today** | The trigger event. Worth more than any demographic |
| **Their words for the desired end state** | Almost never "more leads" |

## Rules once it exists

1. **Verbatim only.** Cleaned-up quotes are paraphrase, and paraphrase is inference again.
2. **Date every quote** and record which ICP it came from.
3. **Never attribute to a named person** without written permission. See [approval rules](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/approval-rules.md).
4. **A quote is evidence of language, not of results.** "It changed my business" is a voice sample, not a testimonial, until permission and substantiation exist.
