# Stats bank

**Every external statistic OS A.I is permitted to cite.** One row each, with its source and date.
If a figure is not here, it does not go in an asset.

**Attribution is mandatory in the copy itself**, not just in this file. "Research shows" is banned —
name the study.

## Speed to lead — evidence for Pillar 1

The strongest external evidence OS A.I has, and it maps directly onto the core argument.

| Stat | Source | Researched |
|---|---|---|
| Contacting a web lead **within 5 minutes** makes qualification **21× more likely** than waiting 30 minutes | Dr James Oldroyd, MIT / InsideSales.com **Lead Response Management Study (2007)** | 2026-08-18 |
| Lead quality **drops ~80% after the first 5 minutes** | Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, *"The Short Life of Online Sales Leads"*, **Harvard Business Review (2011)** | 2026-08-18 |
| Average first-response time across 2,241 audited US companies: **42 hours** | HBR (2011), same audit | 2026-08-18 |
| **23% of audited companies never responded at all** | HBR (2011), same audit | 2026-08-18 |
| Responding within an hour makes meaningful qualification **~7× more likely** than the hour after | HBR (2011) | 2026-08-18 |

**Attribution rules for these:**

- The 21× figure is **MIT/InsideSales (Oldroyd, 2007)** — it is frequently misattributed to HBR. Do not repeat that error.
- The 42-hour, 23% and 7× figures are the **separate HBR 2011 audit**.
- **Both studies are old** — 2007 and 2011. They are still the canonical citations in this field, and being upfront about their age is more credible than hiding it. If a reader checks, they should find exactly what you said.

**Why they matter:** the "23% never responded" figure is the single most useful statistic in this
database. It makes Pillar 1's invisible loss visible with third-party evidence, and it does not
require any OS A.I performance data.

## Tool sprawl and SaaS consolidation — evidence for Pillar 2

**Upgraded to primary sources 2026-09-02, and the direction reversed with them.** The three rows
that stood here until then were aggregator-sourced and are retired at the foot of this section.

| Stat | Source | Researched |
|---|---|---|
| Average applications per organisation **rising again, up 11% year on year**, after two years of falling counts. Mid-market **up 41%, from 116 to 164 apps in a single year**. The report states the consolidation era is over | BetterCloud, *The 2026 State of SaaS Report* | 2026-09-02 |
| Organisations run an average of **27 AI-powered SaaS applications**, about **22% of the portfolio** | same | 2026-09-02 |
| Only **56% of apps in use carry IT approval** | same | 2026-09-02 |
| **36% of SaaS licences go unused** against industry-recommended utilisation levels | Zylo, *2026 SaaS Management Index*, published 29 January 2026 | 2026-09-02 |

URLs: <https://www.bettercloud.com/monitor/the-2026-state-of-saas-report/> ·
<https://zylo.com/news/2026-saas-management-index>
Zylo's index is built on more than **40 million SaaS licences** and **$75 billion** in software
spend under management. Both figures are taken from the publishers' own reports, not from summaries.

**Handle with care.** These describe **companies at large**, including enterprises with IT
departments. A four-person Australian service business is not running 164 apps, and saying so is
stretching the statistic past where it goes.

**Rules:**

- **Never present these as SMB figures.** Say "the average organisation", not "the average small business".
- **Take the mechanism, not the number.** Stacks grow by default; they shrink only when somebody decides to shrink them, and they grow back when that attention moves on. That mechanism transfers to a business with four staff. The app counts do not.
- **The old "counts peaked in 2022 and are falling" line is now wrong.** It was true of the 2024 and 2025 reports and is contradicted by the 2026 one. Do not reuse it. OS A.I's argument does not depend on the trend running one way — the seams cost the same whether the stack is growing or shrinking.
- **The 36% unused-licence figure is the most useful row here**, because the reader can check it against their own stack in an afternoon.
- **Cite Zylo's own announcement.** Secondary pages report 46% and 53% for the same index. That drift is itself an argument for going to primary sources.

**Retired 2026-09-02 — do not reuse:**

| Retired row | Why |
|---|---|
| Average company runs **106 SaaS applications**, down from 112 in 2024 and 130 in 2022 | Aggregator-sourced. Superseded by the BetterCloud 2026 report, which reverses the direction |
| **25–30% of SaaS licences unused or significantly underutilised** | Aggregator-sourced. Superseded by Zylo's primary-sourced 36% |
| Lines of business own **~70% of SaaS spend**; IT controls **~26%** | Aggregator-sourced, and never traced to a report. The nearest primary-sourced equivalent is BetterCloud's 56% IT-approval figure above |

## Australian AI adoption — evidence for Pillar 4

| Stat | Source | Researched |
|---|---|---|
| **~12% of Australian businesses** reported using AI in the workplace in **2024–25** | ABS, *Business adoption of Artificial Intelligence accelerates in 2024–25*, released 25 June 2026 | 2026-09-01 |
| **Small and micro businesses: ~11%.** Medium: **22%** (from 3%). Large: **35%** (from 9% in 2021–22) | same | 2026-09-01 |
| Innovation-active small businesses: **19%** vs **4%** for non-innovation-active | same | 2026-09-01 |
| Top industries: Information, media and telecommunications **38%**; Professional, scientific and technical services **24%**; Financial and insurance services **24%** | same | 2026-09-01 |

URL: <https://www.abs.gov.au/media-centre/media-releases/business-adoption-artificial-intelligence-accelerates-2024-25>
Sample: nearly 7,000 Australian businesses.

**Population note — step 4 of [Adding a stat](#adding-a-stat).** This counts *any* AI use by *any*
Australian business, from a generative-text tool upward. **It is not a measure of AI agents**, and it
must never be presented as one. The right use is scale-setting: most Australian small businesses have
not adopted AI at all yet.

**Rules:**

- Attribute as **ABS**, with the **2024–25** reference period and the **25 June 2026** release date, in the copy itself.
- **Never round 11% up to "one in eight"** or otherwise restate it into a bigger-sounding figure.
- Vendor and industry trackers reporting Australian SMB AI adoption in the **40–70%** range are self-selecting samples measuring "used AI in some form". **They are not interchangeable with this figure and must not be blended with it.** Where the contrast is useful, name both and say why they differ.
- This is a primary source from a national statistical agency, which makes it the strongest external evidence in this database after the speed-to-lead set.

| **10%** of businesses actively collected or analysed data to make informed decisions, **down from 24%** in 2021–22 | ABS, *Characteristics of Australian Business, 2024–25*, released 25 June 2026 | 2026-09-01 |
| **6%** used predictive analysis to plan business activities, **down from 18%** in 2021–22 | same | 2026-09-01 |
| **91%** said their internet met most or all business needs, up from 88% | same | 2026-09-01 |
| **51%** used social media for online presence (was 47%) · **32%** received online orders (was 30%) | same | 2026-09-01 |
| Barriers to ICT use: **insufficient staff skills 16%**, **uncertainty about costs and benefits 13%** | same | 2026-09-01 |
| ICT improved responsiveness to customer needs: **34%** (was 31%) | same | 2026-09-01 |

URL: <https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/technology-and-innovation/characteristics-australian-business/latest-release>
Sample: nearly 7,000 Australian businesses, surveyed October 2025 to February 2026.

**Comparability note — step 4 of [Adding a stat](#adding-a-stat).** The Business Characteristics
Survey was redeveloped for this cycle: the previously alternating Innovation and Digital Activity
modules were combined into one integrated framework on a biennial cycle. The ABS publishes the ICT
year-on-year comparisons itself and reserves its formal caution for the *innovation* series, whose
reference period changed from two years to one. **Cite the 2024–25 levels as levels.** Where the
fall from 2021–22 is used, report it as the ABS reports it and name the redevelopment in the same
passage. Do not describe it as businesses becoming worse at analysis.

**Rules:**

- Attribute as **ABS**, with the **2024–25** reference period and the **25 June 2026** release date, in the sentence.
- These are **all businesses**, not small businesses. Do not restate as an SMB figure.
- This is the first Pillar 2 evidence in the bank from a national statistical agency. Every other tool-sprawl figure here is aggregator-sourced and company-wide.

## Adding a stat

1. Find the **primary source**, not an article citing it
2. Record the publisher, the study name and the year
3. Add the URL and a `researched:` date
4. Note who the population actually is — this is where most statistical misuse happens
5. Then it may be cited, with attribution in the copy
