# Playbook — blog and articles

## Purpose

Own the arguments in the [nine clusters](../11-topic-clusters/README.md) so that a reader who has
never heard of OS A.I finishes a piece thinking differently about their own business. **Articles are
the primary asset this database was built to produce.** They publish to the site's **A.I News**
section.

## Formats and lengths

| Type | Words | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard article** | 900–1,400 | One argument, properly made. The default |
| **Pillar / long-form** | 1,800–2,500 | A whole cluster topic. Rare — only where depth is genuinely needed |
| **Explainer / SEO** | 800–1,200 | Answers one question completely. Definitional |
| **Short opinion** | 500–800 | One sharp argument, no build-up |

## The universal structure

1. **Land in a situation.** Specific, concrete, time-stamped. Never a rhetorical question
2. **Make the invisible cost visible.** With a sourced number where one exists
3. **The reframe.** What they believe → what they should believe
4. **The mechanism.** How it actually works. Named, specific
5. **The honest limit.** What this does not solve. **Mandatory**
6. **One CTA**

## Do

- Fill the brief first. It catches errors before they are written
- Attribute every statistic **in the sentence** — study, publisher, year
- Byline it: **Tumai** for argument and economics, **Ariki** for delivery reality
- Vary paragraph length deliberately
- Argue from mechanism when no evidence exists — it is often stronger anyway
- Link to related articles in the same cluster
- Australian English throughout

## Don't

- No rhetorical-question openers
- No "In today's fast-paced business world"
- No summary paragraph restating the piece
- No performance claim about OS A.I
- **Never name GoHighLevel**
- No statistic without a source and date in the copy
- No rule-of-three cadence, no "not just X — it's Y"
- No em-dash pile-ups. Two per piece
- Never map to two pillars

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Brief completed and every field filled
- [ ] Exactly one messaging pillar
- [ ] Every fact traces to a database file
- [ ] Every stat attributed in the copy, with year
- [ ] No claim outside the register
- [ ] No OS A.I performance claim
- [ ] GoHighLevel not named; no false provenance
- [ ] Banned language Tiers 1–3 checked explicitly
- [ ] Australian English, no emoji, no exclamation marks
- [ ] Paragraph lengths vary
- [ ] Honest-limit section present
- [ ] One CTA
- [ ] Byline assigned
- [ ] Approval tier identified
- [ ] Sources statement attached

## Worked example — opening 150 words

Article: *"23% of businesses never reply at all."* Brief:
[worked example](../12-content-engine/worked-example-brief.md).

> An enquiry lands at 7:14 on a Tuesday evening. Someone has found your website, read enough to be
> interested, and filled in a form. They are, at that moment, the warmest they will ever be.
>
> You see it at 9:05 the next morning. You reply within the hour, which feels responsive.
>
> By then they have messaged two other businesses. One of them replied at 7:16.
>
> You will never know this happened. That is the part worth sitting with — the lead does not tell
> you they went elsewhere, they simply do not reply, and a fortnight later you conclude it was a
> tyre-kicker. Nothing in your CRM records it. Nothing in your reporting shows it. The only visible
> symptom is that the month was quieter than it should have been, and the explanation you reach for
> is that you need more leads.
>
> In 2011, Harvard Business Review audited 2,241 companies…

**Why this works:** specific time, follows one enquiry, makes the invisible loss visible, names the
misdiagnosis, and arrives at the sourced statistic having earned it. No rhetorical question, no
banned language, no claim about OS A.I.
