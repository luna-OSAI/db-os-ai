# Playbook — newsletter

## Purpose

Weekly authority. One idea, well made, to a list that has not bought yet. **The newsletter is not a
digest of company news** — it is an argument, delivered regularly enough to build a habit.

## Format

**400–700 words. One idea. Zero or one link.**

| Section | Words |
|---|---|
| Subject line | 4–8 words, sentence case |
| Opening — a situation or a claim | 80–120 |
| The idea, developed | 200–350 |
| The honest limit or counter-case | 60–100 |
| Close — a thought or one small action | 50–80 |

## Do

- **One idea per issue.** A second idea is next week's issue
- Write from Tumai or Ariki, as a person
- Recycle a cluster argument — the newsletter is where article arguments get tested before they become articles
- A specific number, attributed, where one exists
- **Functional unsubscribe, every issue** — Spam Act 2003
- Consistent day and cadence. The habit is the asset

## Don't

- No roundups of company news
- No "in this issue" tables of contents
- No multiple links competing for the click
- No performance claim
- **Never name GoHighLevel**
- No urgency or scarcity framing
- No emoji, no exclamation marks

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] One idea
- [ ] 400–700 words
- [ ] Subject line 4–8 words, sentence case
- [ ] Every number attributed with its source and year
- [ ] No claim outside the register
- [ ] Honest limit or counter-case present
- [ ] Zero or one link
- [ ] **Unsubscribe present and functional**
- [ ] Sender identification accurate
- [ ] Banned language Tiers 1–3 checked
- [ ] Australian English, no emoji, no exclamation marks

## Worked example — opening

> **Subject:** the automation nobody is watching
>
> A client showed me their setup last month. Nineteen automations, built over two years, all of them
> sensible when they were built.
>
> Six had stopped working.
>
> Not broken loudly — nothing errored, nothing alerted. A field name changed in one tool, and the
> connector that depended on it quietly stopped passing data. The follow-up sequence it triggered
> simply never ran, for four months.
>
> This is the thing about duct-taped systems that nobody mentions when they sell you the duct tape…
