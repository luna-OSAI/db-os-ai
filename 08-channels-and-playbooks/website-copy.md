# Playbook — website and landing pages

## Purpose

Convert a visitor who arrived with a problem into someone who books or starts a trial, without
overclaiming. The site's existing nav is **Chat Agent · Voice Agent · Free Blueprints · A.I News ·
Login**, with plans at $97 / $297 / $497.

## Page types and lengths

| Page | Length | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Homepage** | 400–700 words | Name the problem, name the offer, one primary CTA |
| **Product page** (Chat Agent, Voice Agent) | 600–1,000 | One product, one buyer, one action |
| **Landing page** (single campaign) | 400–800 | **One offer, one CTA, no nav** |
| **Pricing page** | 300–500 + table | Clarity above persuasion |
| **Lead magnet page** | 200–400 | One promise, one form |

## The universal structure

1. **Headline** — the problem or the transformation. Magician register
2. **Subhead** — the mechanism, in one sentence. Ruler register
3. **The problem, specifically** — three beats, concrete
4. **The mechanism** — how it works, named
5. **What it replaces** — [what we replace](../02-offer-and-pricing/what-we-replace.md). The most concrete proof available
6. **Proof** — Tier B mechanism proof. **Not invented results**
7. **Objection handling** — the two or three that actually block
8. **Pricing or CTA** — one primary action

## Do

- **One page, one buyer.** The SMB owner and the agency founder need different pages — [positioning](../04-voice-and-messaging/positioning.md) warns that mixing them makes both weaker
- Headline Magician, body Ruler
- Name the tools being replaced, specifically
- Put the price on the pricing page. Hiding it costs more trust than it saves
- **State the trial auto-conversion wherever the trial appears**
- Australian English

## Don't

- No feature dumps
- **No invented results, client counts or testimonials**
- **Never name GoHighLevel**, never claim to have built the platform
- No "risk-free" — the trial converts and fees are non-refundable
- No "no refunds" phrasing that implies the buyer has no ACL rights
- **No tier difference you cannot name** — gap G12
- No partner income implication
- No hostile competitor framing

## The registered site claims

C1–C5 in the [claims register](../05-proof-and-evidence/claims-register.md) — "10× Faster", "Join
100s of entrepreneurs", "$50,000+ pre-built business kit", "We cover $5,000/m in plugins" and "Loved
By All Industries" — are **approved for reuse on the site as published**, per decision D2. **C6 is
not approved.**

**Do not escalate them, do not attach them to a customer, and do not build arithmetic on them.**
When a page needs proof, reach for Tier B mechanism proof first — it is more persuasive to this
buyer and it survives scrutiny.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] One buyer, one page
- [ ] One primary CTA
- [ ] Headline names the problem or the change, not the feature
- [ ] Mechanism stated in plain language
- [ ] Every claim in the register, in registered wording
- [ ] No invented proof
- [ ] GoHighLevel not named; no false provenance
- [ ] Trial auto-conversion disclosed if the trial appears
- [ ] No refund phrasing implying loss of rights
- [ ] No unnamed tier difference
- [ ] Banned language Tiers 1–3 checked
- [ ] Australian English, no emoji, no exclamation marks
- [ ] Approval tier identified

## Worked example — product page opening

> ## Your phone rings while you're on a job. That's the end of it.
>
> OS A.I answers, qualifies the caller and books them into your calendar — then texts you what
> happened.
>
> Not a voicemail. Not a message you return tomorrow, by which point they have called someone else.
> A conversation, at the moment they were interested enough to pick up the phone.
>
> **What it replaces:** the receptionist you can't justify, the missed-call voicemail nobody
> returns, and the follow-up that depends on you remembering.

**Why it works:** headline lands in a specific situation and states the loss. Subhead is the
mechanism, concretely. The replacement framing is checkable. No performance claim, no invented
result, no banned language.
