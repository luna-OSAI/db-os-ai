# Playbook — email

## Purpose

Move someone from problem-aware to booked, without pressure. **Education first** — the marketing
plan specifies 30–60 day nurture sequences with no hard selling, and that is the right instinct for
a buyer who has been sold to badly before.

## Formats and lengths

| Type | Words | Asks |
|---|---|---|
| **Welcome / lead magnet delivery** | 80–120 | 1 — deliver the thing |
| **Education** | 120–200 | 0–1 |
| **Article notification** | 60–100 | 1 — read it |
| **Objection / FAQ** | 120–180 | 0–1 |
| **Re-engagement** | 80–120 | 0–1. Give something; never ask why they went quiet |
| **Booking invitation** | 80–120 | 1 |
| **Newsletter** | 400–700 | See [newsletter](newsletter.md) |

## The universal structure

1. **Open at them, not at us.** No "I hope this finds you well." No "At OS A.I we believe"
2. **One idea.** A second idea is a second email
3. **Something concrete** — a step, a scenario, a sourced number
4. **One ask, at the end, at most**
5. **Plain sign-off** from Tumai or Ariki, as a person

## Do

- Write to one person. Contractions, short paragraphs
- Subject lines **4–8 words, sentence case.** No colon-stacking, no fake "Re:"
- Australian English
- Attribute every number
- **Functional unsubscribe in every message** — Spam Act 2003
- **Accurate sender identification** — Spam Act 2003
- Send from a person, not "The OS A.I Team"

## Don't

- No emoji. No exclamation marks
- **No manufactured urgency, countdowns or fake scarcity.** The generic template packs in source material are full of this. It is not this brand
- No rhetorical-question openers
- No "Quick question" as a subject line
- **No performance claim.** No "our clients see…"
- **Never name GoHighLevel**
- **No partner income implication, ever** — including in a customer email that mentions partnering
- Never re-summarise something they already have

## Trial and billing emails — extra care

Any email touching the 7-day trial **must** state that it converts to a paid subscription unless
cancelled, and how to cancel. Any email touching refunds must not imply the recipient has no rights.
[ACL and consumer law](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/acl-and-consumer-law.md).

## Pre-send checklist

- [ ] Under the word limit for its type
- [ ] Opens at the reader
- [ ] One idea, one ask
- [ ] Exactly one messaging pillar
- [ ] Every claim traces to the [claims register](../05-proof-and-evidence/claims-register.md)
- [ ] Every number carries its source
- [ ] Banned language Tiers 1–3 checked
- [ ] Subject line 4–8 words, sentence case
- [ ] Australian English, no emoji, no exclamation marks
- [ ] **Unsubscribe present and functional**
- [ ] **Sender identification accurate**
- [ ] Trial auto-conversion disclosed, if relevant
- [ ] No partner income implication
- [ ] Passes: would you say this out loud to a friend?

## Worked example — education email

> **Subject:** the leads that never complain
>
> Most businesses I talk to think they have a lead generation problem.
>
> Usually they have a response problem. In 2011 Harvard Business Review audited 2,241 companies and
> found the average first response to a web enquiry took 42 hours. Twenty-three per cent never
> responded at all.
>
> The reason it persists is that it is invisible. A lead who goes cold does not send a complaint —
> they just stop replying, and three weeks later you file them as not serious. Nothing in your
> reporting records what happened.
>
> Worth checking your own: submit an enquiry through your own website this week and time the reply.
> Most people are surprised, and not in a good way.
>
> Tumai

**Why it works:** opens at the reader, one idea, sourced and attributed with the year, an ask that
costs nothing and creates the realisation itself, no claim about OS A.I at all.
