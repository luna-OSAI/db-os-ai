# Playbook — social distribution and cadence

> **Status: `draft`.** Needs review before it governs anything. The account facts below are observed
> and dated. The benchmark figures are not sourced yet and are recorded as gaps.

## Purpose

[`social.md`](social.md) governs **what a post says**. It does not cover what happens after the post
exists. This file covers that: which account publishes, in what format, how often, and how it
reaches anyone at all.

Both files apply to every social asset. Where they conflict, `social.md` wins on craft and this file
wins on operations. Compliance outranks both.

## The observed position, 2026-10-01

Audited directly from the public accounts on 2026-10-01.

| Observation | Value |
|---|---|
| facebook.com/oscaleai followers | 33 |
| Facebook posting pattern | Static graphics, consistent, approximately six months |
| Reported result | No measurable reach over that period |
| youtube.com/@Oscale_A.I | 31 videos, 11 subscribers |
| TikTok | @oscale40 |
| LinkedIn company page | Unconfirmed, recorded as gap S2 |

Two things follow from that table, and neither of them is a content-quality problem.

**A page with 33 followers has no audience to publish to.** Whatever the per-post reach rate turns
out to be, a small percentage of 33 is a number close to zero. Six months of consistent posting into
that base behaved the way the arithmetic predicts.

**A static image has no route to someone who does not already follow the page.** Facebook serves
unconnected reach mainly through short video. An image on a small page is shown to a fraction of
existing followers and stops there.

The six months were not effort applied badly. They were effort applied to the step after the one
that was missing.

## Rule 1 — publishing is not distribution

Budget roughly a third of the time on making the post and two thirds on putting it in front of
people. Where that is not possible, publish less often rather than distribute less.

The mechanism is worth stating plainly, because it explains why volume has not worked. Early
engagement decides whether a post is shown to a second and larger group. A post that lands flat in
its first hour does not get that second group, and no amount of posting frequency substitutes for
it.

## Rule 2 — the account architecture

[`social.md`](social.md) already rules that posts go out **as Tumai or Ariki personally, not as a
brand page.** This file does not soften that. It adds what the pages are for.

| Account | Job | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Tumai / Ariki personal, LinkedIn | Primary. Most of the reach, both ICPs | 4–5 posts/week |
| Tumai / Ariki personal, Facebook | Primary for the SMB owner | 3–4 posts/week |
| OS A.I Facebook page | Credibility. Someone who hears the name checks it, and it has to look current | 3–4 posts/week, plus every short video |
| OS A.I LinkedIn page | Reshares the personal post later with one added line | 2 posts/week |

The page is read by people deciding whether OS A.I is real. That is a genuine job and it is the
reason to keep posting there. It is not the reason anyone will see the post.

## Rule 3 — format mix

### Facebook

| Format | Share | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Short video, 9:16, under 45 sec | 50% | The only format on a small page with a route to non-followers |
| Text post | 20% | No asset needed, and the comments are what carry it |
| Carousel | 15% | Holds attention longer than a single image |
| Single graphic | 10% | One strong number only. No longer the default |
| Link post | 5% | Lowest reach on the platform |

### LinkedIn

| Format | Share | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Text post | 45% | Highest-reach format, and the one `social.md` is already written for |
| Document or carousel PDF | 25% | Time spent on the post is the signal that matters |
| Native video, under 90 sec | 20% | The same cut as the Facebook video, re-exported 4:5 |
| Single image | 10% | |
| Link in the post body | 0% | Suppresses reach. First comment instead |

### Short video specification

- 9:16, 1080×1920, under 45 seconds
- Readable text on screen within the first two seconds, and visible movement
- Burned-in captions always, since most viewing is muted
- A face or a screen in frame. A static graphic with music behind it is still a static graphic, and
  the platform treats it as one
- Uploaded natively, never carrying another platform's watermark
- **For any clip of the chat or voice agent working, no background music.** The agent is the point,
  and music over it removes the only evidence the clip contains

## Rule 4 — the launch sequence

Run this on every post.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Before posting | Comment on several posts in the niche first |
| On posting | Publish |
| Within minutes | Share to the personal profile with a line of context (Facebook) |
| Within minutes | Link as the first comment, if there is a link at all |
| Within the hour | Send it to a handful of people it genuinely applies to, with a real sentence |
| Within the hour | Post the value-only version into relevant Facebook groups, with no brand and no link |
| First 90 minutes | Reply to every comment, as a person |
| Next day | Return to the best thread and continue it |

**On group posting:** [`social.md`](social.md) rules that we never pitch in someone else's group, and
that holds without exception. The value version answers the question and stops. Where a group member
asks what we use, that is the moment it is reasonable to say, and not before.

## Rule 5 — comments before posts

For an account this size the order of value is:

1. Comments on other people's posts, where an audience already exists
2. Answers in Facebook groups
3. Direct replies to people who engaged
4. Our own posts

Our own post is fourth until the first three have built something for it to land on. That is
uncomfortable and it is the actual position.

## Rule 6 — the back catalogue

**31 videos exist on YouTube and have been seen by very few people.** On Facebook and LinkedIn that
material is new. It is the largest unused asset the brand currently has.

Each long video yields roughly three short vertical clips, one LinkedIn-native cut, two text posts
drawn from its strongest argument, and one carousel where it contains a list.

Cut on these four moments only:

1. A number said out loud
2. A contradiction, meaning any point where the argument turns against the obvious
3. A screen visibly performing a task
4. A sentence someone might disagree with

Skip intros, context-setting and sign-offs. Start the clip on the point.

Work oldest first. Those have the fewest existing views, so they are the most genuinely new.

## Cadence

| Day | Personal LinkedIn | Personal and page Facebook |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Argument post | Short video |
| Tue | Document or carousel | Text post |
| Wed | Story post | Short video |
| Thu | Native video | Carousel |
| Fri | Contrarian post | Short video |
| Sat | — | Short video, re-cut of the week's best |

**Weekly production block, about two hours.** Cut four short videos from the back catalogue, write
the week's posts against a cluster, build one carousel, schedule everything, and fill in last week's
numbers.

Hold volume here until the launch sequence is habitual. More posts without distribution lowers the
average engagement rate, which is the figure the ranking responds to.

## Measurement

Track these. Follower count below roughly a thousand is noise.

| Signal | Why |
|---|---|
| Conversations started | The only one that precedes revenue |
| Saves and shares | Predicts onward reach better than likes |
| Video hold rate in the first few seconds | Tells you whether the opening line works |
| Profile and page visits from a post | Measures curiosity rather than politeness |
| Share of video reach that is non-followers | The purpose of the format change, stated as a number |

**Attribution.** Social attribution is unreliable and UTMs will miss most of it. Ask on the call and
write the answer down. That record will beat any dashboard built for this.

**Review monthly.** Where a format has had six honest attempts and not moved, stop making it. Where
a post worked, run it again several weeks later in different words, because the reach was never the
same people twice.

## Compliance notes specific to this channel

- **Partner and commission content is out of scope for social.**
  [`partner-and-income-claims.md`](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/partner-and-income-claims.md) is
  `draft`, and its interim rules state that partnership is not marketed to general audiences and that
  recruitment is never the lead. A public feed is a general audience. No partner-facing post is
  generated from this playbook while that file remains `draft`. That includes posts stating
  commission percentages without converting them to dollars, because on a public feed the
  percentage is the recruitment pitch.
- **No fabricated client stories.** A post describing a client, a tool count, a figure saved or a
  response time achieved is a claim about a result, and none exists.
  [`what-we-cannot-claim.md`](../05-proof-and-evidence/what-we-cannot-claim.md) applies in full.
  Where a post needs a concrete detail, use a sourced external statistic, or write the scenario
  plainly as a scenario.
- **No engagement bait.** `social.md` bans "comment YES for the guide" and "Thoughts?" endings.
  Nothing in the distribution plan above depends on either. Posts close on the idea, or on something
  the reader can go and check for themselves.
- **GoHighLevel is never named**, including in a comment, a reply or a group answer.
- No emoji, no exclamation marks, Australian English. This applies to captions and comments as much
  as to the post itself.
