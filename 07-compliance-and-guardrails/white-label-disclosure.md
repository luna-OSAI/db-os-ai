# White-label disclosure

## The rule

**GoHighLevel is never named client-facing.** Always "OS A.I" or "the platform."

The company states this as FIRM. It predates this database and is not up for negotiation in a draft.

## Where it applies

Everywhere a customer, prospect, partner or member of the public could see or hear it: website,
ads, email, SMS, social, blog articles, sales calls, chat and voice agent responses, proposals,
onboarding material, support replies, partner assets.

It applies to **indirect identification too** — no GHL screenshots with visible branding, no
GoHighLevel help-doc links, no "if you've used HighLevel before…", no support instructions naming
the underlying system.

## Where it does not apply

Internal documents, this database, technical implementation notes, and conversations with the
platform vendor. **The white-label is not a secret from the team — only from the market.**

## Why the rule exists, and its limits

White-labelling is standard practice in this category and is not deceptive in itself. The customer
buys OS A.I's configuration, agents, setup and support — that is genuinely what they are paying for,
and it is genuinely OS A.I's work.

**But the rule has a boundary, and it is worth being precise about it.** Not naming a supplier is
fine. Making an affirmative false statement about the technology is not — that is s18 territory. So:

- ✅ "The platform" · "our platform" · "OS A.I"
- ✅ "We built and configured the system for you"
- ✅ Describing capabilities as things OS A.I provides
- ❌ "We built this platform from scratch"
- ❌ "Our proprietary technology" / "our own AI models"
- ❌ "Software we developed in-house"
- ❌ Any claim of having engineered the underlying platform

**Silence about the vendor is the rule. Fabricating provenance is not covered by it** — and it would
also be the kind of claim that unravels badly if a technical buyer recognises the interface, which
a meaningful share of agency-owner prospects will.

## If asked directly

Prospects sometimes ask what the platform is built on. The answer is not a denial:

> "We license and configure enterprise infrastructure rather than rebuilding what already works —
> what you're buying is the system we build on top of it: the agents, the automations, the setup
> and the support."

True, non-evasive, does not name the vendor, and reframes onto what OS A.I actually sells. Never
deny that a third-party platform is involved.
