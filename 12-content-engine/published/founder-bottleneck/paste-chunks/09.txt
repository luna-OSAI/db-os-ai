<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">A documented process only helps if the team follows it. <p style="margin:0 0 14px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">Systemising the founder bottleneck fixes a capacity problem. It does not fix a demand problem. If the real issue is that not enough enquiries arrive, faster follow-up on a thin pipeline will show you that more clearly rather than change it. It does not fix an offer the market does not want, and it will not rescue pricing that never worked.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 14px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">It also does not touch the keep list from Step 2. Those decisions stay yours, and the time you free up is meant to go there.</p>
<p style="margin:0 !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">And it will not work on a business where nobody is willing to let a decision be made differently from how you would have made it. If every call your team makes without you gets reversed, the authority is decorative and the escalations will come straight back. That part is not a systems problem.</p>

<details style="border:1px solid #DDE5EE !important;border-radius:8px !important;background:#F4F7FA !important;margin:0 0 10px !important;overflow:hidden !important">
<summary style="cursor:pointer !important;padding:17px 22px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-weight:600 !important;font-size:20px !important;line-height:1.3 !important;color:#002147 !important">What is a founder bottleneck?</summary>
<div style="padding:0 22px 20px !important"><p style="margin:0 !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:17.5px !important;line-height:1.55 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">A founder bottleneck occurs when business operations, decisions or growth depend too heavily on the founder's personal time, knowledge or approval. It is a structural condition rather than a personal failing, and it usually accumulates one reasonable decision at a time.</p></div>
</details>
<details style="border:1px solid #DDE5EE !important;border-radius:8px !important;background:#F4F7FA !important;margin:0 0 10px !important;overflow:hidden !important">
<summary style="cursor:pointer !important;padding:17px 22px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-weight:600 !important;font-size:20px !important;line-height:1.3 !important;color:#002147 !important">How do I know if I am the bottleneck?</summary>
<div style="padding:0 22px 20px !important"><p style="margin:0 !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:17.5px !important;line-height:1.55 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">Look for recurring patterns where work stops when you are unavailable, team members regularly ask for approval, or you are personally handling repeatable tasks that could follow a defined process. <summary style="cursor:pointer !important;padding:17px 22px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-weight:600 !important;font-size:20px !important;line-height:1.3 !important;color:#002147 !important">At what size does the founder bottleneck become a problem?</summary>
<div style="padding:0 22px 20px !important"><p style="margin:0 !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:17.5px !important;line-height:1.55 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">Earlier than most advice assumes. Coverage of this topic tends to focus on companies of 50 people or more, but a business with three or four staff hits the same ceiling, because it has no management layer to absorb decisions. The fix is different at that size: the available owner for routine work is a system rather than a new manager.</p></div>

<summary style="cursor:pointer !important;padding:17px 22px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-weight:600 !important;font-size:20px !important;line-height:1.3 !important;color:#002147 !important">Is being the founder bottleneck the same as micromanaging?</summary>
<div style="padding:0 22px 20px !important"><p style="margin:0 !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:17.5px !important;line-height:1.55 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">No, and the difference decides the fix. Micromanaging is a behaviour, which means staying involved in work someone else owns. A founder bottleneck is a structure, where the business routes decisions through one person because it has no other way to resolve them. A founder can stop micromanaging entirely and still be the bottleneck.</p></div>
