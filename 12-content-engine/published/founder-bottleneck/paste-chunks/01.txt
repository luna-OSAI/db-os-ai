<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important" id="osai-body-start">A quote request lands at 7:40 on a Tuesday night. You see it Wednesday, somewhere between a site visit and the job you promised for Friday. You reply Thursday morning.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">By then four more things are queued behind it. A discount someone needs approved. A reschedule nobody will confirm without you. A customer who rang your mobile directly, because they always have.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">None of that is a failure. It is a founder bottleneck, and it is the most common reason a growing business stops feeling like it is growing.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">It usually starts for a good reason. You built the business. You know the customers. You know how the work gets done. In the early stages, keeping everything close to you makes the business faster.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">The problem comes when the business grows and the operating model does not. Your team has grown. Your customer base has grown. The number of decisions has multiplied. And the business still runs through one person.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">The result is predictable. Work waits. Decisions pile up. Your calendar fills with operational questions instead of the work that actually changes something.</p>
<p style="margin:32px 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:clamp(21px,2.5vw,27px) !important;line-height:1.32 !important;font-weight:600 !important;color:#002147 !important;border-left:3px solid #007FFF !important;padding:2px 0 2px 20px !important">Working harder deepens the dependency, because the more you personally handle, the more only you can handle.</p>
