<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">A standard operating procedure helps, and a list of steps is rarely enough on its own. Your team also needs the logic behind the decision.</p>
<p style="margin:0 0 24px !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:18px !important;line-height:1.62 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">Instead of documenting "ask the founder before offering a discount", write the actual rule:</p>

<p style="margin:0 !important;font-family:'Barlow Condensed',sans-serif !important;font-size:19.5px !important;line-height:1.5 !important;color:#2D2D2D !important">Discounts up to <strong style="color:#002147 !important;font-weight:700 !important">10%</strong> can be approved by the sales lead when the job is over <strong style="color:#002147 !important;font-weight:700 !important">$5,000</strong>, the customer has paid on time before, and the work is scheduled more than <strong style="color:#002147 !important;font-weight:700 !important">two weeks</strong> out. Anything beyond 10%, or any new customer, comes to me.</p>

You have turned personal knowledge into an operating rule.</p>
