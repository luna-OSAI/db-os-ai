<div class="lf-step-head lf-rev" id="step-3"><p class="lf-step-mark">Step 3 <span class="lf-dots" aria-hidden="true"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class=""></i><i class=""></i></span> <span class="of">of 5</span></p><h2>Build the First-Response Automation</h2></div>
<p>The first-response automation is the workflow that runs the moment someone enquires. Interest is usually highest right when a prospect reaches out, so this is where speed pays off. If you want the case for why timing matters so much, read <a href="https://www.oscale.ai/post/speed-to-lead-why-the-first-five-minutes-decide-who-gets-the-job">Speed to Lead: Why the First Five Minutes Decide Who Gets the Job</a>.</p>
<div class="lf-stat lf-rev" role="figure" aria-label="Lead response findings from the 2011 Harvard Business Review audit"><div class="lf-stat-bar"></div><div class="lf-stat-in"><div class="lf-stat-figs"><p class="lf-stat-fig"><b class="lf-num" data-count="42" data-suffix=" hours">42 hours</b><span>Average time to a first response, across 2,241 audited companies</span></p><p class="lf-stat-fig"><b class="lf-num" data-count="23" data-suffix="%">23%</b><span>Of those companies never responded at all</span></p></div><p class="lf-stat-src">Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, &ldquo;The Short Life of Online Sales Leads&rdquo;, <em>Harvard Business Review</em> (2011). The canonical studies in this field are old, 2007 and 2011, and they are still the ones the industry cites.</p></div></div>
<p>A basic first-response workflow does five things:</p>
<ul class="lf-cells lf-kids" aria-label="First-response workflow"><li class="lf-cell" style="--i:0"><span class="n">1</span><h3>Creates or updates the contact</h3><p>One record per lead, always current.</p></li><li class="lf-cell" style="--i:1"><span class="n">2</span><h3>Sends an immediate reply</h3><p>SMS or email, straight away.</p></li><li class="lf-cell" style="--i:2"><span class="n">3</span><h3>Asks a few qualifying questions</h3><p>Only a small number.</p></li><li class="lf-cell" style="--i:3"><span class="n">4</span><h3>Directs qualified prospects to the right calendar</h3></li><li class="lf-cell" style="--i:4"><span class="n">5</span><h3>Notifies your team</h3><p>When human attention is required.</p></li></ul>
<p>An <a href="https://www.oscale.ai/chat">AI chat agent</a> can carry this conversation around the clock. It answers common questions, qualifies the enquiry and helps book the appointment, so the conversation starts at once instead of hours later when someone spots a form submission. If your leads also call, an <a href="https://www.oscale.ai/voice">AI voice agent</a> covers the phone in the same way.</p>
<p>Do not automate everything. Automate the predictable steps and set clear handoff points for the conversations your team should handle personally, such as complex quotes or upset customers.</p>

<div class="lf-step-head lf-rev" id="step-4"><p class="lf-step-mark">Step 4 <span class="lf-dots" aria-hidden="true"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class=""></i></span> <span class="of">of 5</span></p><h2>Create a Follow-Up Sequence</h2></div>
<p>One message is rarely a complete follow-up system. Most people who enquire do not book on the first reply, so build a short sequence for the ones who go quiet. Mix helpful reminders, answers to common questions and a clear call to action.</p>
<div class="lf-card lf-rev"><div class="lf-panel-bar"></div><div class="lf-panel-in"><p class="lf-kicker">A sequence over one week</p><h3>Five touchpoints, five reasons to reply</h3><div class="lf-track lf-rev" aria-hidden="true"><div class="lf-track-line"><div class="lf-track-fill"></div><i class="lf-dot" style="left:0%;--p:0"><span>Day 0</span></i><i class="lf-dot" style="left:14.29%;--p:.143"><span>1</span></i><i class="lf-dot" style="left:42.86%;--p:.429"><span>3</span></i><i class="lf-dot" style="left:71.43%;--p:.714"><span>5</span></i><i class="lf-dot" style="left:100%;--p:1"><span>7</span></i></div></div><div class="lf-tablewrap"><table><thead><tr><th scope="col">Day</th><th scope="col">Message</th><th scope="col">Purpose</th></tr></thead><tbody class="lf-kids"><tr style="--i:0"><td>Day 0</td><td data-label="Message">Immediate acknowledgement and booking link</td><td data-label="Purpose">Confirm you received the enquiry and show the next step</td></tr><tr style="--i:1"><td>Day 1</td><td data-label="Message">Quick check-in asking if they have questions</td><td data-label="Purpose">Remove small blockers</td></tr><tr style="--i:2"><td>Day 3</td><td data-label="Message">Helpful answer to a common objection or problem</td><td data-label="Purpose">Give them a reason to reply</td></tr><tr style="--i:3"><td>Day 5</td><td data-label="Message">Reminder with another chance to book</td><td data-label="Purpose">Make booking low effort</td></tr><tr style="--i:4"><td>Day 7</td><td data-label="Message">Final check-in before moving them to longer-term nurture</td><td data-label="Purpose">Close the loop politely</td></tr></tbody></table></div></div></div>
<p>Avoid sending the same &ldquo;just following up&rdquo; message five times. Each touchpoint should give the prospect a reason to respond, whether that is an answer, a useful detail or an easier way to book.</p>
<p class="lf-flow lf-rev"><strong>Build in one rule from the start: when a lead books, the sequence stops.</strong> Nothing puts off a new customer faster than a reminder to book an appointment they have already booked.</p>
<p>OS A.I can run these workflows across connected channels, so follow-up stays consistent without anyone having to remember it.</p>
