## 3 · The argument

> **Most businesses lose more revenue to slow replies than to weak marketing — and because a cold
> lead never complains, the loss is invisible, so the money goes into buying more leads instead of
> answering the ones already paid for.**

**The reframe:** *"I need more leads"* → *"I'm already losing the ones I have, and I can't see it."*

## 5 · Approved evidence

| Stat | Source + date | Attribution in copy |
|---|---|---|
| **23% never responded** to a web enquiry | HBR 2011, audit of 2,241 companies | "a Harvard Business Review audit of 2,241 companies in 2011" |
| Average first response: **42 hours** | HBR 2011 | Same sentence |
| **5-minute reply = 21× more likely to qualify** than 30 minutes | MIT/InsideSales, Oldroyd 2007 | "Dr James Oldroyd's MIT and InsideSales study" — **not HBR** |
| Lead quality drops ~80% after 5 minutes | HBR 2011 | "the same HBR research" |

⚠️ **The 21× figure is MIT/InsideSales, not HBR.** It is routinely misattributed. Do not repeat that.
⚠️ **State the study dates.** 2007 and 2011. Being upfront about their age is more credible than
hoping nobody checks.

## 6 · Must NOT claim

- No OS A.I customer results, response times or lead volumes
- No "never miss another lead" — that is a performance claim
- No implication that these study figures are OS A.I outcomes
- No competitor named
- No client count — **"Join 100s of entrepreneurs" is registered but belongs on the site, not in an awareness article** where it would read as a results claim
- No timeline guarantee

## 7 · Structure

| Section | Job | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | The 7pm enquiry, followed hour by hour | 150 |
| The number | 23% never replied. 42-hour average. Named, dated | 250 |
| Why it's invisible | A cold lead never complains. It never appears in a report | 250 |
| What it costs | Reframe: you already paid for that lead twice | 200 |
| The mechanism | Instant reply, in-conversation booking, missed-call trigger | 150 |
| **The honest limit** | This does not get you more leads. If nobody is enquiring, this is the wrong fix | 100 |
| Close | One ask: check your own response time this week | 80 |

## 8 · Objections to pre-empt

- "I do respond quickly" → during business hours
- "My leads aren't time-sensitive" → the audit spanned sectors
- "I don't want a robot talking to my customers" → touch briefly; do not resolve it here

## What this example demonstrates

**The brief did work the writer would otherwise have done badly.** It caught the misattribution
between two studies that are constantly conflated, it blocked "never miss another lead" before it
was written, and it kept a registered site claim out of a context where it would have read as a
results claim.

None of that requires the writer to know the compliance rules. **It requires them to fill in the
brief.**
