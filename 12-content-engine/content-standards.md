# Content standards

The rules every piece obeys, whatever the channel.

## The five that matter most

### 1 · One piece, one argument
If it takes two sentences to say what the piece argues, it is two pieces. Multi-argument content
converts nobody because it commits to nothing.

### 2 · Every fact has a home
Nothing is asserted that does not trace to a file. **A number without a source file does not ship.**
See [claims policy](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/claims-policy.md).

### 3 · Attribution lives in the copy
Not in a footnote, not in this database — **in the sentence.** "The MIT/InsideSales study found…"
not "research shows…". Vague attribution is a banned AI tell and a credibility leak.

### 4 · Name the limit
Every substantial piece says what it does not solve. This is the credibility mechanism that replaces
case studies OS A.I does not have.

### 5 · Mechanism over transformation
Magician brands fail by being vague — the shadow archetype is literally the con artist. **Wonder has
to be specific.** Say how, not just what changes.

## Format standards

| Format | Words | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Article** | 900–1,400 | Full brief structure |
| **Long-form / pillar** | 1,800–2,500 | For genuinely deep cluster topics only |
| **Newsletter** | 400–700 | One idea, one link |
| **LinkedIn post** | 100–250 | One idea, no link in body |
| **Email** | 80–200 | One idea, one ask |
| **SMS** | Under 160 chars | One ask, opt-out |
| **Landing page** | Varies | See [website copy](../08-channels-and-playbooks/website-copy.md) |

## Structural rules

- **Headings are sentence case.** Never Title Case
- **Vary paragraph length.** Uniform three-sentence paragraphs is the loudest AI tell
- **Bold the load-bearing sentence** per section, not decoratively
- **Lists only for genuinely parallel items.** Not as a substitute for prose
- **No summary paragraph** restating what was just said
- **Tables where the content is genuinely tabular**, not to look organised

## The opening

The first 100 words decide it. Rules:

- ❌ **No rhetorical question.** "Tired of losing leads?" is the most exhausted opener in the category
- ❌ No "In today's fast-paced business world"
- ❌ No dictionary definitions
- ❌ No "we've all been there"
- ✅ Land in a specific situation — a time, a scenario, a concrete detail
- ✅ Or state the argument flatly and defend it
- ✅ Or open on a number that has a source

## Attribution to a person

Articles are bylined **Tumai** or **Ariki**, deliberately:

| Topic | Byline |
|---|---|
| Market argument, positioning, systems thinking, economics | Tumai |
| Delivery reality, what breaks, sales conversations, practical how-to | Ariki |

Founder-led content is one of the few assets a company without case studies actually has. **Use it
rather than writing in a corporate voice.**

## What gets rejected

- Any claim outside the [claims register](../05-proof-and-evidence/claims-register.md)
- Any statistic without a source and date **in the copy**
- Any mention of GoHighLevel, or implied false provenance
- Any partner income implication
- Any performance claim about OS A.I
- Tier 1 banned language
- Copy a competitor could put their logo on
- Anything with no honest-limit section
