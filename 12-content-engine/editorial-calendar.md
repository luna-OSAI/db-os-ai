# Editorial calendar — Q4 2026

> **Status: `draft`.** Needs review before it governs anything. Every idea here is drawn from
> [idea bank](idea-bank.md). This file schedules them. It does not add any.

**Period:** Mon 6 Oct to Fri 18 Dec 2026. Eleven working weeks. The AU market winds down from about
the third week of December, so the quarter is planned to finish on 18 December rather than run to 31.

Depends on [social-distribution](../08-channels-and-playbooks/social-distribution.md) for cadence,
format mix and the launch sequence. Depends on [social](../08-channels-and-playbooks/social.md) for
craft.

## The strategy in one page

### One pillar per month, not four per week

[Messaging pillars](../04-voice-and-messaging/messaging-pillars.md) rules that every asset maps to
exactly one of four. The temptation is to rotate all four weekly. At this account size that is the
wrong call for two reasons.

Repetition is what makes an argument stick when few people see any single post. And rotating four
arguments at once means that when something finally works, there is no way to tell which argument did
it.

So each month concentrates on one pillar, and the contrarian slot runs across all of them.

| Month | Pillar | Why this one, this month |
|---|---|---|
| **October** | **1** — the gap between enquiry and reply | Strongest external evidence in the database, and the only pillar whose proof needs no OS A.I performance data. Correct for a cold audience |
| **November** | **2** — the stack is the problem | The argument competitors structurally cannot make. Highest commercial intent, and it carries the HubSpot comparison |
| **December** | **4**, closing on **3** | End of year is when owners ask whether next year looks like this one. "Could you take two weeks off" lands in an Australian December in a way it does not in March |

**Pillar 3 is deliberately held to December.** It answers the stall, not the cold reader, so it is
wasted on an audience that has not yet accepted pillars 1 and 2.

### The quarter has a second job

[Enrichment roadmap](../00-start-here/ENRICHMENT-ROADMAP.md) item 1 is to record five discovery
calls, and it is ranked first because **not one sentence of real customer language exists in this
database.** Every hook, objection and angle currently in use is the team's guess.

That makes this quarter's real constraint clear. The three highest-value pieces in
[idea bank](idea-bank.md) are blocked, and they are blocked on the same thing: no consenting client.

So the calendar is built to produce that material as a by-product rather than wait for it:

- Every conversation that starts from a post is a discovery call candidate. Record it, with consent.
- The Wednesday story slot is the destination for what those calls teach, de-identified.
- By December the hooks bank should be getting rewritten from real language. That single change will
  improve every asset in this calendar more than any scheduling decision in it.

**Record the baseline before the first changed post.** Gap S3 in
[social-distribution](../08-channels-and-playbooks/social-distribution.md). Without week-zero numbers
there is no way to show whether any of this worked.

### What is not in this quarter

**No partner content at all.**
[partner-and-income-claims](../07-compliance-and-guardrails/partner-and-income-claims.md) is `draft`,
and its interim rules put partnership out of scope for a public feed. That removes the most obvious
hook for the agency founder, so the agency founder is served through pillars 2 and 3, repeatability
and the cost of change, for the whole quarter. This is a constraint, not an oversight, and it closes when
roadmap item 3 closes.

**No client results, no case studies, no figures about what OS A.I achieved.** None exist.

## The nine weekly engines

Ninety unique ideas is not a plan anyone executes. Nine repeatable slots is.

| # | Engine | Slot | Pillar | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **Sourced stat** | LinkedIn Mon | month's | [stats-bank](../06-research-and-market/stats-bank.md) only |
| E2 | **Carousel or document** | LinkedIn Tue | month's | the month's cluster |
| E3 | **Operator story** | LinkedIn Wed | month's | [origin-story](../01-company/origin-story.md), [how-we-work](../01-company/how-we-work.md), and discovery calls once recorded |
| E4 | **Screen clip** | LinkedIn Thu + FB | month's | the live agents, recorded |
| E5 | **Contrarian** | LinkedIn Fri | varies | the contrarian set in [idea-bank](idea-bank.md) |
| E6 | **Back-catalogue cut** | FB Mon/Wed/Sat | month's | the 31 YouTube videos |
| E7 | **Diagnostic question** | FB Tue | month's | the question openers in [hooks-and-openers](../09-content-banks/hooks-and-openers.md) |
| E8 | **Group answer** | daily, no post | any | whatever was actually asked |
| E9 | **Article amplification** | on merge | that article's | the `article/*` branches |

### Notes on the engines that carry the most weight

**E1 — sourced stat.** The HBR 2011 and MIT 2007 figures are the strongest evidence in the database
and the only concrete numbers available without a client. Attribution goes in the copy, naming the
study, per guardrail 7. The 21× figure is MIT/InsideSales and is widely misattributed to HBR;
do not repeat that error. Both studies are old, and saying so is more credible than hiding it.

**E4 — screen clip.** This is the only format that demonstrates the product without making a claim
about it. A recording of the agent answering, qualifying and booking is evidence of capability, which
is permitted, and it is not a performance claim, which is not. No music over the agent's voice.
Before any clip of a live agent ships, run roadmap item 4 and put the seven questions to Oliver and
Olivia. A clip of an agent saying something non-compliant is worse than no clip.

**E5 — contrarian.** Eight pieces exist in the set, which covers eight of the eleven weeks.
[idea-bank](idea-bank.md) is explicit that for a business with no case studies this is the fastest
available trust mechanism, and it disqualifies bad-fit readers before a sales call. Treat the Friday
slot as the most important post of the week.

**E8 — group answer.** Not a post, and the likeliest source of an actual conversation this quarter.
Answer the question and stop. Never pitch. Where someone asks what we use, that is the moment, and
not before.

## October — Pillar 1

Argument: most deals are not lost to marketing, they are lost in the hours between someone raising
their hand and someone answering. Cluster: [speed-to-lead](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-speed-to-lead.md),
with [crm](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-crm.md) in week 4.

| Week | E1 Mon stat | E2 Tue carousel | E5 Fri contrarian |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W1** 6–11 Oct | HBR 2011: 23% never replied | What happens to a Saturday enquiry | **You don't need more leads** |
| **W2** 13–18 Oct | HBR 2011: 42-hour average first response | The five places an enquiry can land, and who watches each | **When you don't need a CRM** |
| **W3** 20–25 Oct | MIT 2007: five minutes versus thirty, 21× | Speed to lead, what the research actually says | **Why the spreadsheet won** |
| **W4** 27 Oct–1 Nov | HBR 2011: within the hour, ~7× | Your CRM has contacts in it from eighteen months ago | **Your CRM isn't a discipline problem** |

**Anchor piece:** idea bank #1, *23% of businesses never reply at all*. Strongest evidence, zero
compliance risk, and it is already ranked first in the suggested five. Publish the article, then run
E1 and E6 off it for a fortnight.

**W4 ties to the in-flight `article/crm-discipline-problem` branch.** When that article merges, the
Friday contrarian and the Monday stat should both point at it. That is E9 working as intended.

**The October close.** Every post this month can end on the same action, and it is the best close
available because the reader proves the argument to themselves: submit an enquiry through your own
website tonight and time the reply. No CTA, no engagement bait, and it generates the DM.

## November — Pillar 2

Argument: eight to twelve tools that do not talk to each other cost more than their subscriptions.
Clusters: [software-sprawl](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-software-sprawl.md),
[duct-taped-systems](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-duct-taped-systems.md),
[cost-savings](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-cost-savings.md).

| Week | E1 Mon stat | E2 Tue carousel | E5 Fri contrarian |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W5** 3–8 Nov | A quarter to a third of licences paid for and unused | Add up your software bill. All of them | **In defence of Zapier** |
| **W6** 10–15 Nov | SaaS counts peaked in 2022 and are falling | How to tell if your systems are actually connected | **When consolidation is the wrong answer** |
| **W7** 17–22 Nov | HubSpot Professional $890/mo, onboarding $3,000 separate | What the stack costs that the invoices do not show | **When switching isn't worth it** |
| **W8** 24–29 Nov | Return to the unused-licence figure, applied | Your automations are broken and nobody told you | **Some things shouldn't be automated** |

**Two compliance notes for this month, both load-bearing.**

The SaaS figures describe companies at large, not one-to-ten-person service businesses. Never present
them as SMB figures, and prefer the direction to the number. An SMB running 106 apps is not credible
and claiming it damages everything around it.

The HubSpot comparison is **Tier 2 approval** and needs a dated price re-check before publication.
Comparison is fine; a hostile frame is not, per guardrail 8.

**Anchor piece:** idea bank #2, *What HubSpot Professional actually costs*. Highest commercial intent
in the bank. **W8 ties to the in-flight `article/eleven-good-tools-is-not-a-tech-stack` branch.**

**The November close:** count your tools, then check which ones anyone opened last week.

## December — Pillar 4, closing on Pillar 3

Argument: nobody started a business wanting a sixty-hour week, and the founder became the bottleneck
by accident. Clusters: [founder-bottleneck](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-founder-bottleneck.md),
[ai-for-small-business](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-ai-for-small-business.md), then
[automation](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-automation.md) and
[workflows](../11-topic-clusters/cluster-workflows.md).

Pillar 4 carries a warning in the pillars file and it applies all month. "Without you" must never
read as "without control". The stated want is time back without losing quality. Overshoot and it
triggers the fear instead of the purchase.

| Week | E1 Mon | E2 Tue carousel | E5 Fri contrarian |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W9** 1–6 Dec | ABS 2024–25: ~11% of small and micro businesses reported using AI | Could you take two weeks off | **Hiring moves the bottleneck. It doesn't remove it** |
| **W10** 8–13 Dec | ABS: 19% of innovation-active small businesses versus 4% | You bought the platform and never finished setting it up | **"AI for your business" is not a job** |
| **W11** 15–18 Dec | Recap the quarter's evidence in one post | Our onboarding, all ten steps | **What not to automate** |

**On the ABS figure.** Attribute as ABS, reference period 2024–25, released 25 June 2026, in the copy.
It counts any AI use by any Australian business, so it is a scale-setting number and never a measure
of AI agents. Do not round 11% up to "one in eight". Do not blend it with vendor surveys reporting
40–70%.

**Two transparency pieces land this month, and both do double duty.**

*Our onboarding, all ten steps* publishes the real SOP. It is proof, positioning and a lead magnet at
once, and it is the clearest answer to Pillar 3's objection without promising a timeline.

*Should the AI say it's an AI?* is the strongest piece available to a company with no case studies,
because nobody else in the category writes it. It requires roadmap item 4 to be done first, and it
closes gap G15. Schedule the agent test in W9 so the post can run in W11.

**December tapers.** W11 finishes on 18 December. Do not post into the last ten days of the month.
Use that time for the quarter review and for the January plan.

## Lead generation — the honest mechanism

Social at this account size will not produce a meaningful number of inbound leads this quarter, and
a plan that implies otherwise is not worth following. The path that does work, in order:

1. **Group answers** generate the first conversation. E8, daily.
2. **The self-diagnosis close** gives the reader a reason to message. They checked their own reply
   time, they did not like the answer, and now there is something to talk about.
3. **The two lead magnets**, the connected-systems checklist in W6 and the onboarding SOP in W11,
   convert a reader into a contact.
4. **Every conversation is a discovery call candidate.** With consent, recorded. See roadmap item 1.

No target numbers are stated here. Guardrail 6 keeps internal targets out of customer-facing
material, and inventing projections for an internal plan would be invention either way. What to watch
is in [social-distribution](../08-channels-and-playbooks/social-distribution.md) under Measurement.

## Monthly review

End of each month, before planning the next:

- [ ] Fill in the measurement signals. Conversations started is the one that matters
- [ ] Which argument produced conversations, not which post produced likes
- [ ] Did the contrarian slot outperform the others, as [idea-bank](idea-bank.md) predicts
- [ ] Were any discovery calls recorded. If none, that is the finding
- [ ] Did every post run the launch sequence. If not, the month's data means little
- [ ] Any new claim that crept in. Check against [claims-register](../05-proof-and-evidence/claims-register.md)
